**AI Email Manager** is the section of Callin.io where your AI stops making calls and starts handling email. To open it, click the product switcher at the top of the left sidebar and choose **AI Email Manager**. It works around three areas:

- **Mail Agents**: where you create and configure the agents that manage your mail.
- **Email**: where you read and reply to the messages your agents have processed.
- **Tools**: where you connect third-party services that give your agents extra powers.

A **Mail Agent** is an assistant that watches an inbox for you. It reads each new message, works out what it's about, and prepares a reply following your instructions. You decide how far it goes: it can leave a draft for you to review, or send the reply on its own. Several agents can share the same inbox and split the work between them.

Setting up your first agent takes about ten minutes, and you don't need any technical background.

> **Note:** AI Email Manager is marked as **Beta**. It's still growing, so some screens may look slightly different in your account.

## What you need before you start

- The email account you want the agent to work with. It can be Gmail, Outlook, or almost any other provider.
- If you're not using Gmail or Outlook and your account has two-factor authentication, an **app password**: a special password your provider generates for external apps, so you never share your real one.
- A rough idea of what the agent should do. One sentence is enough, for example: "answer questions about orders and pass anything about refunds to a person".
- Credits in your account. Each AI draft uses credits (see [Credits for AI drafts](/Mail-Agent-Email#credits-for-ai-drafts)).

You don't need the inbox ready right now. You can build the agent first and connect the inbox later.

## Step 2 — Tell the agent how to behave

Open the **Agent** tab. This is where you shape the agent's personality and judgement.

### Conversation Prompt

The **Conversation Prompt** is where you write, in plain language, how the agent should handle email. There's no special syntax: write it the way you'd brief a new colleague.

A short example:

> Respond professionally and forward billing questions to a human.

The more precise you are, the more predictable the results. A more useful version for an online store: "You answer on behalf of Brightside Home, an online store for home goods. Be friendly and brief. Answer questions about orders, shipping and returns. If a customer asks for a refund, say a member of the team will reply within one business day. Never promise delivery dates."

The counter in the bottom right shows how much you've used of the 4000 characters. Saved changes apply to new incoming mail.

> **Tip:** Write down what the agent must **not** do, not just what it should do. Sentences like "never confirm appointments", "never promise refunds" or "never discuss prices over email" prevent most surprises.

### AI Intelligence (LLM)

On the right you'll see **AI Intelligence (LLM)**. An **LLM** (large language model) is the engine that reads the email and drafts the answer.

Click the model card to open the **LLM** panel, then click the model to see the choices. Next to each model you'll see its reasoning effort, **(Low)** or **(Medium)**: how much the model "thinks" before writing. More effort brings more nuance, but also more time and cost per reply.

| Model | Reasoning effort | What it's good for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| GPT 5.4 Mini | Low | Best default for quality, speed and cost |
| Claude Sonnet | Medium | Polished, natural email tone |
| GPT 5.4 Nano | Low | Fastest and lowest-cost option |
| GPT 4.1 Mini | Low | Stable, balanced option for high volumes |

New agents start with **GPT 4.1 Mini**.

In the same panel you'll find **Temperature**, a slider from 0 to 1 that starts at 0.7. Lower values keep the wording consistent and repeatable. Higher values make it more varied and natural, but less predictable.

> **For beginners:** You don't have to touch any of this. If replies feel robotic, try Claude Sonnet or raise the temperature a little. If they vary too much, lower it.

## Step 3 — Organise your mail with categories

Open the **Categories** tab. Categories are optional labels that tell the agent what kind of message it's dealing with: refunds, order status, complaints, and so on. Each category appears as a card with its badge and description, a pencil icon to edit it and a bin icon to delete it.

They let you treat each kind of email differently. For example, the agent can reply on its own about order status, but only prepare a draft when the message is about money.

Click **Add category**. A window opens with these fields:

| Field | What it does | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Category name | The short label you'll see on each email | Order status |
| Description | Tells the agent when to use this category | Customers asking where their order is, when it will ship or how to track it |
| Badge color | The colour of the label | Green |

You can pick one of the preset colours or create your own with the **\+** swatch.

Below the colours there are two switches:

- **Generate draft reply for this category**: on by default. The agent prepares a reply and leaves it ready for you to review.
- **Auto-reply for this category**: off by default. The agent sends the reply itself, with no review.

If you switch both off, the agent only labels the message (**Categorize only**) and writes no reply. That's useful for mail a person should always handle, like complaints or legal matters.

Click **Add category** to add it. Each category needs both a name and a description.

> **Common mistake:** Writing a vague description, like "important stuff". The agent uses this text to decide which emails belong here, so describe the actual content: "messages asking for an invoice, a refund, or a change of payment method". If emails land in the wrong category, tightening the description is usually the fastest fix.

> **Important:** Start with **Generate draft reply** on and **Auto-reply** off. Read the drafts for a few days. Once the agent gets the tone and the facts right, turn on automatic replies where mistakes would be harmless.

## Step 4 — Connect the inbox

Open the **Mailbox** tab. This is where you link the email account the agent will look after. Until you do, the agent is configured but idle.

You have three options:

- **Gmail**: connect with your Google account, on Google's usual permission screen.
- **Outlook**: connect with Microsoft 365, in the same way.
- **Other email (IMAP & SMTP)**: connect any other provider. **IMAP** lets the agent read your inbox and **SMTP** lets it send replies. Your provider publishes both settings in its help pages.

If you choose **Other email (IMAP & SMTP)**, the **IMAP / SMTP setup** form asks for:

| Field | What to enter |
| --- | --- |
| Email address | The inbox the agent will read from and send from |
| Password or app password | Your password, or better, an app password if your account has two-factor authentication |
| IMAP host | The reading server, such as imap.yourprovider.com |
| IMAP port | Usually 993, filled in by default |
| Days of mail to import | How far back the agent looks when you first connect: 1 to 45 days (30 by default) |
| SMTP host | The sending server, such as smtp.yourprovider.com |
| SMTP port | Usually 587, filled in by default |

Click **Connect mailbox**. Your credentials are encrypted and stored securely. Callin.io then imports the messages from the chosen period (**Syncing your inbox**). Until the sync finishes, new messages may take a moment to appear.

> **Note:** You can skip this step and connect the inbox later. Everything else stays saved, but the agent won't process anything until an inbox is linked.

## Step 5 — Fine-tune the behaviour (optional)

Open the **Advanced** tab. Everything here is optional, and both rules start switched off.

### Automation rules

**Reply only to a specific sender.** Switch it on and type an address in **Sender email address**. The agent then processes and replies only to that address, using your prompt. Other senders are ignored, and categories become optional. It's useful for single-purpose automations, and it's the safest way to test an agent in a live inbox: point it at your own address and watch what it does.

**Copy automatic replies.** Sends a copy of every automatic reply to an extra address, which you type in **Copy recipient email**. Under **Delivery type** you choose:

- **CC (visible)**: the person who receives the reply can see that address.
- **BCC (hidden)**: the copy is sent silently.

This is handy when a manager wants to keep an eye on what the agent sends.

> **Tip:** If you enable automatic replies in any category, enable the copy as well, at least while you're building trust in the agent.

> **Warning:** If the copy goes to a colleague, choose **BCC (hidden)**. With **CC (visible)**, your customer sees your colleague's address in every message.

### Agent priority

When several agents share the same inbox, the first agent that matches an email handles it. Use the arrows to change the order.

Order them from most specific to most general: an agent restricted to a single sender should sit above a general support agent.

You can only change the order once an inbox is connected. Until then, the screen shows **Connect an inbox on the Mailbox tab to manage agent priority**.

## Step 6 — Save your changes

When you have unsaved changes, a bar appears at the bottom of the screen with two buttons: **Discard**, which undoes your changes, and **Save**, which stores them.

> **Important:** Don't leave the screen while that bar is visible. If you navigate away without clicking **Save**, your changes are lost.

## The Email section: read and reply in one place

**Email** is the unified inbox: the messages processed by your agents, from every connected mailbox, with the category each one received. It's where you review, edit and send the drafts your agents prepare, and where you can generate replies with AI. See [Email](/Mail-Agent-Email).

## The Tools section: give your agents superpowers

**Tools** is where you connect third-party services that give your agents live data, such as the **Amazon Seller API** to look up orders and add tracking links to replies. See [Tools](/Mail-Agent-Tools).

## How to check it worked

Three quick signs that everything is in place:

1. In the **Mail Agents** list, your agent no longer shows **No email assigned** under its name.
2. Send a test email to the connected inbox from another address of yours, using wording that matches one of your categories.
3. Wait a few minutes and open [Email](/Mail-Agent-Email). Depending on the category, you should find a draft ready to review or a reply already sent.

If nothing happens, check in order:

- That the inbox really is connected and has finished syncing.
- That **Reply only to a specific sender** isn't on and pointing at a different address.
- That your test email matches a category description. An automatic reply only goes out when the message falls into a category with auto-reply on.
- That you have credits, or that your free trial is still active.

## What to do next

- Add a couple more categories now that you've seen the agent in action. Real emails always suggest labels you hadn't thought of.
- Visit **Email** every day during the first week to review the drafts and polish the Conversation Prompt.
- Take a look at [Tools](/Mail-Agent-Tools) from time to time: new integrations can appear as the product grows.
