## What White Label means in Callin.io

White Label is how you make Callin.io feel like **your own product** to your customers.

Instead of users seeing “Callin.io” everywhere, you can shape a customer-facing experience that matches your brand: the **name of the app**, the **domain they log into**, the **default language**, and other trust and billing-related details. The goal is simple: when your client signs in, it feels like they’re using _your platform_, not a third-party tool.

A practical example: if you’re an agency running calling operations for multiple clients, White Label helps you present a clean, consistent experience—so onboarding, daily usage, and customer emails all look like they belong to your brand.

> **Important:** Some options may vary depending on your plan or permissions, so what you see can differ slightly from another account.

## How to reach White Label settings (the part many people miss)

Most customers don’t find White Label on the first try because it lives inside **Console**, not in the “day-to-day” app screens.

To get there:

- Open your Callin.io app.
- Click **Console** in the **top-right corner**.
- In Console, open the White Label area from the left sidebar.

> **Tip:** If you can’t see the **Console** button, you likely don’t have the right role. Ask the account owner/admin to grant you access.

## The White Label sections you’ll see in Console

When you open White Label in Console, you’ll notice there are multiple sections in the left sidebar. This page is a “map” so you know what each area is for before you start changing anything.

### White Label Configuration

This is the main hub where you define the core of your branded experience.

Inside it, you’ll typically see:

- **Configuration**: the practical settings (name, domain, language, region, policy links, and similar basics).
- **Appearance**: the branding layer (visual identity options). We’ll cover it in a dedicated guide—here we’re just setting expectations.

### White Label Domains

This section is focused on domains: what domains are connected, and the steps involved in making a custom domain work correctly.

Think of it as the place you come back to when you’re managing, verifying, or reviewing your domain setup.

### Integrations

This is where you connect your White Label experience with other tools you use day-to-day (for example, services that support your workflows).

You don’t need to set anything here to understand White Label—but it’s good to know it exists if you want a more complete branded operation later.

### Users

If multiple people manage the account, **Users** is where access is handled.

It matters because White Label work is often done by admins or by someone responsible for branding and settings.

### Subscriptions and subscription plans

White Label isn’t only “look and feel.” It also touches customer-facing billing context.

In these areas, you typically manage things like:

- your active subscription
- the plans you offer or reference (depending on how your account is configured)

### Features and capabilities

This is the “what’s enabled” area—where you can review what features are available for your account.

It’s useful context, because some White Label options may depend on what’s enabled in your plan.

### UTM Analytics

If you do marketing or run campaigns, **UTM Analytics** is where tracking and attribution can live.

Not required for White Label basics, but it can be part of delivering a polished, measurable customer experience.

### Content management (Guides, FAQs, templates)

You may also see content-related sections (like **Guides**, **FAQs**, and **Agent Templates**).

This matters for White Label because the more your customer-facing experience is branded, the more helpful it is to also brand the learning experience—what customers read when they need help.

> **Tip:** Don’t worry if you don’t plan to use every section. Most teams start with Configuration + Domain, then expand later.

## What you can configure on this first page (high level)

On the White Label Configuration screen (Configuration tab), you’re looking at the foundation. The goal here is to understand what each option _represents_, without doing the step-by-step yet.

### App Name

This is what users will recognize as the name of your platform inside the interface (and in some customer-facing places, like page titles and emails).

Example: instead of “Callin.io,” you might set it to **“Northwind Voice Portal.”**

### Custom Domain (and DNS configuration)

This is where you connect a domain you control so customers log in through your brand.

You’ll also see a **DNS Configuration** action—this is the piece that helps your domain correctly point to the platform. We’ll cover the exact steps later; for now, it’s enough to know this is part of “making the domain real.”

### Currency

This sets the default currency used for plan prices and billing-related context.

If you sell to customers in a specific region, this helps everything feel consistent and expected.

### App Language

This sets the default language of the app experience for your users.

It’s one of the quickest wins for customer comfort—especially if you support teams in multiple countries.

### Server location

This lets you choose where data is hosted, which can matter for compliance, latency, or customer requirements.

### Privacy Policy

This is a trust detail: it’s where you configure the policy link or related customer-facing legal information.

It’s not “branding” in the visual sense, but it’s part of presenting a complete, credible product.
