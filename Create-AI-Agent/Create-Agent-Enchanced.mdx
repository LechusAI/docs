[Open the AI Agents page in Callin.io](https://app.callin.io/agents)

The **AI Agents** page is where you create and manage the agents that handle your phone calls and website conversations. From here you can create a new agent, find an existing one, test it, and open its editor.

Each agent's editor is organized in nine tabs at the top: **Agent**, **Workflow**, **Knowledge Base**, **Conversation**, **Analytics**, **Tools**, **Call Action**, **Website widget**, and **Advanced**. This page explains how to create an agent and what you can do in each tab.

### The agents list

Every agent in your account appears as a row with its name, the phone number assigned to it (or *No phone number assigned*), its call direction, its language, and the date it was created.

- **Search agents…** — find an agent by name.
- **Direction** — show only **Inbound**, **Outbound**, or **Both** agents.
- **Sort** — order the list by **Newest first**, **Oldest first**, **Name A–Z**, **Name Z–A**, or **Custom order**.

On the right of each row you have three quick actions:

- **Test Call** — place a test call with the agent.
- **Test AI agent** — talk to the agent from your browser.
- **More options** (⋮) — **Copy ID**, **Clone**, **Edit**, **Assign Number**, **View events calendar**, and **Delete**.

<Tip>
  Use **Clone** to try changes on a copy before touching an agent that's already taking real calls.
</Tip>

### Create a new agent

Click **Create Agent** at the top right. Callin.io asks how you want to start.

<Frame caption="Screenshot: choose how to start your new agent.">
  <img
    src="/images/ai-agents-new-agent.png"
    alt="The New Agent screen with three options: Start from Scratch, Browse our Templates, and Predefined Templates." />
</Frame>

**Start from Scratch** builds an agent with a basic prompt and greeting that you then shape in the editor. Choose it when you already know what the agent should do. You only need to enter the **Agent Name** (up to 50 characters) and choose the **Agent stack**.

**Browse our Templates** starts from a ready-made agent designed for a specific business and use case. It's the fastest way to get a working first version:

<Steps>
  <Step>
    **Industry** — pick the one that best describes your business: Retail & E-commerce, Healthcare & Medical, Finance & Banking, Real Estate, Education & Training, Hospitality & Travel, Automotive, Professional Services, Technology & Software, Government & Public, Food & Beverage, Manufacturing, Fitness & Wellness, Legal Services, Non-Profit, or Media & Entertainment.
  </Step>
  <Step>
    **Use case** — choose what the agent will help with. The options depend on the industry. For example, Healthcare & Medical offers Customer Support, Appointment Scheduling, Patient Intake, Insurance Verification, Prescription Reminders, and more.
  </Step>
  <Step>
    **Complete your agent** — enter the **Agent Name**, choose the **Agent stack**, and fill in:
    - **Website** *(optional)* — your website address. Callin.io only reads publicly available information from it to personalize the agent.
    - **Main Goal** — in up to 300 characters, what the agent should achieve on each call.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Frame caption="Video: Browse our Templates → industry → use case → Complete your agent.">
  <video autoPlay muted loop playsInline src="/images/ai-agents-create.mp4" />
</Frame>

<Tip>
  Write the **Main Goal** as one clear outcome, for example "Book, confirm, or reschedule patient appointments and answer questions about opening hours." The clearer the goal, the better the first version of the prompt.
</Tip>

**Predefined Templates** *(Beta)* uses templates created by your own organization, so every new agent starts with your company's standard setup. Each card shows the template's name, a short description, and how many fields it pre-fills (for example *Agent Name*, *Prompt*, *Greeting Message*, *Direction*). Select one, review the **Agent Name**, choose the **Agent stack**, and click **Create Agent**. If your organization hasn't created any templates yet, this list is empty.

### Choose the agent stack

The **Agent stack** decides which voice and AI technology powers the agent:

- **Premium Voice** — natural Callin.io voices, ready to deploy in minutes. This is the default and the right choice for most agents.
- **Custom Stack** *(Alpha)* — bring your own LLM, TTS, and STT for full control over each part of the voice pipeline.
- **Ultra-low latency** — near-instant responses for smooth, live conversations.

Click **Create Agent** to finish. The agent opens directly in its editor, on the **Agent** tab.

<Warning>
  The sections below describe an agent created with the **Premium Voice** stack. Agents on other stacks may show different options.
</Warning>

### Saving your changes

When you change anything in the editor, a bar appears at the bottom with the message **You have unsaved changes**:

- **Save** — stores all your changes.
- **Clear** — discards every unsaved change and restores the last saved version.

While there are unsaved changes, **Test Call** is disabled. If something needs fixing before saving (for example, an incomplete workflow), the bar tells you what's missing or shows a **View Errors** button.

<Frame caption="Screenshot: the unsaved changes bar. Save to keep your changes, or Clear to discard them.">
  <img
    src="/images/ai-agents-unsaved-changes.png"
    alt="The agent editor with the You have unsaved changes bar and the Clear and Save buttons at the bottom." />
</Frame>

### Testing your agent

Use the buttons at the top right of the editor to test without leaving the page:

- **Test Call** — places a real call with the agent.
- **Test AI agent** — lets you talk to the agent from your browser.
- **Console** — opens the developer console.

<Tip>
  Walk through the tabs in order the first time, and test after every important change. A good setup must sound right on a real call, not just look right in the editor.
</Tip>

## Section 1: Agent

The **Agent** tab is where you define the core identity and behavior of your assistant: what it says, which voice and language it uses, which AI model powers it, and whether it answers incoming calls, makes outgoing ones, or both.

<Frame caption="Screenshot: the Agent tab, where you set the prompt, voice, language, model, and call direction.">
  <img
    src="/images/agentesia2.png"
    alt="The Agent tab showing the Conversation Prompt editor on the left and the Voice, Language, AI Intelligence (LLM), and Call Direction panels on the right." />
</Frame>

### Conversation Prompt

The **Conversation Prompt** is the main set of instructions your agent follows during a call: the goals it must achieve and the guardrails it must respect to stay on-brand and compliant. Think of it as the agent's playbook.

A new agent starts with a template organized in three sections you should adapt:

- **Personality** — who the agent is and the role it plays.
- **Environment** — the context it operates in (support call, appointment booking, reception, and so on).
- **Tone** — how it should sound (calm, friendly, professional, brief).

After those three, add operational rules that guide the agent's decisions during the call. Some that work well for most setups:

- Ask only one clarifying question at a time.
- Always move the user toward the next useful step.
- Use the knowledge base for reference answers.
- Keep responses short unless the user asks for detail.
- Don't invent answers. If the information is unclear, say so.

<Tip>
  Write the prompt as if you were briefing a new employee on their first day. Operational and clear, not decorative.
</Tip>

### Add Variables and Dynamic Variables

The **Add Variables** button below the prompt editor (and below the greeting) inserts placeholders that are replaced with real values during the call. The menu has two groups:

- **System Variables** — values Callin.io fills in automatically: `system__agent_id`, `system__called_number`, `system__time_utc`, `system__time`, `system__timezone`, and `system__call_id`.
- **Custom Variables** — your own variables, such as `agent_name`. Click **New Variable** to create one.

Variables appear as chips below the editor and in the **Dynamic Variables** panel at the bottom of the tab, where you set their value. For example, `{{agent_name}}` is set to the agent's name by default.

This is the right approach when you want to reuse the same agent for different brands, locations, or services without rewriting the prompt every time.

### Time zone

Next to **Add Variables** there's a time zone selector (for example, **Rome (GMT+1)**), with a search box to find any city. This is the time reference the agent uses when it talks about *today*, *tomorrow*, *this afternoon*, or any specific time during the call.

Always set it to the real time zone of the business the agent represents. A wrong time zone leads to wrong appointment times.

### Default personality

The gear icon next to the time zone opens the **Default personality** toggle. It's off by default. When you turn it on, Callin.io adds a set of general guidelines to the prompt, for example: don't repeat who you are unless asked, give helpful and informative answers, ask clarifying questions when needed, and be polite, professional, clear, and concise.

Turn it on if your prompt is short and you want these good practices added for you. If your prompt already covers them, leave it off.

### Greeting Message

The **Greeting Message** is the very first thing the agent says when the call starts. A good greeting tells the caller who they're talking to and what kind of help is available, in one short sentence. You can use variables here too, for example `Hi, this is {{agent_name}}—thanks for calling, what can I do for you today?`

A few examples:

- Support: "Hi, I'm the support assistant. Tell me what's going on and I'll help you step by step."
- Appointments: "Hi, I'm the booking assistant. I can help you schedule, change, or cancel an appointment."
- Reception: "Hi, thanks for calling. I'm the virtual assistant and I'll help you with your request."

### Allow Interruptions

**Allow Interruptions**, next to the greeting, lets callers interrupt the agent while it's delivering the first message. It's on by default, which feels natural for most support, reception, and customer service flows.

<Warning>
  **Common mistake:** Leaving interruptions on when your greeting includes information the caller must hear in full, such as a legal notice or a recording disclaimer. In that case, turn it off.
</Warning>

### Voice

**Voice** controls how your agent sounds. The voice doesn't change what the agent says, but it heavily affects how callers feel during the call — clarity, trust, and overall impression.

Click the selected voice (marked **Primary**) to open the voice panel:

- **Voice** — click it to open **Select Voice**, which has three tabs:
  - **Explore** — the catalog of available voices, with a search box and filters by language and gender.
  - **Cloned** — voices you've cloned in your account.
  - **Add** — import a voice from ElevenLabs by pasting its VoiceLab share link and giving it a name.
- **Expressive Mode** — on by default. Lets the agent express speech with audio tags, like an emotion, a speed, or an accent.
- **Suggested audio tags** — up to 20 tags the voice can use for emotional nuance, such as *Excited*, *Concerned*, *Patient*, *Whispering*, *US accent*, or *Laughing*. Click a tag to add it and describe when the agent should use it, or click **Add** to create your own. The tags are suggestions: the agent can also use tags not listed.

### Add additional voice

Click **Add additional voice** to attach secondary voices, useful when one agent needs to handle several languages or different customer segments. For each one you set:

- **Voice** — the voice to use.
- **Label** — a unique identifier the model uses to refer to this voice.
- **Language** — the language for this voice (by default, *Same as agent*).
- **Description** — when the agent should use this voice, for example "For any Spanish words" or "Whenever Joe is speaking."

Click **Add voice** to save it.

### Common voice settings

The gear icon next to **Voice** opens **Common voice settings**, two audio controls shared by all the agent's voices:

- **TTS output format** — the format of the generated audio: **PCM 8000 Hz**, **PCM 16000 Hz** (*Recommended*), **PCM 22050 Hz**, **PCM 24000 Hz**, **PCM 44100 Hz**, **PCM 48000 Hz**, or **ulaw 8000 Hz** (*Telephony*). PCM 16000 Hz is a good balance between audio quality and processing speed.
- **Optimize streaming latency** — a scale from **0** to **4** (default **3**). Higher values make the agent start speaking faster, at a slight cost in audio quality.

### Language

**Language** sets the agent's default language (marked **Default**). Click it to choose another one from the searchable list.

Click **Add Additional Languages** to pick the other languages the agent can switch to during the conversation. The panel has a search box and a **Select all languages** option.

Only enable the languages you actually need. Adding extra languages "just in case" tends to make behavior less predictable. To switch languages automatically, the **Language Switching** system tool must also be on (see **Section 6: Tools**).

### AI Intelligence (LLM)

**AI Intelligence (LLM)** is the language model that powers the agent. The **LLM** (Large Language Model) is what understands the caller, follows the rules in your prompt, uses the knowledge base, and generates each response.

Click the selected model to open the **LLM** panel, and click it again to see the list. Each model shows a short description and its approximate latency, like *~179ms*. The models available are **Neuron 1.0**, **Neuron 2.0**, **Neuron 3.0**, **GPT-OSS 120B**, **GLM-4.5 Air**, **GPT-4.1 Nano**, **GPT-5 Nano**, **Gemini 2.5 Flash Lite**, and **Gemini 2.5 Flash** (the default).

The model directly affects three things:

- How well the agent understands what the caller means.
- How fast it answers.
- How consistent it stays across long conversations.

For appointment-focused agents and scheduling flows, **Gemini 2.5** is a solid practical choice.

### Thinking Budget and Temperature

The **LLM** panel also includes two settings:

- **Thinking Budget** — controls how many internal reasoning tokens the model can use before responding. Off by default. More reasoning can improve complex answers but adds latency, so test it on real calls.
- **Temperature** — controls how creative or deterministic the responses are (default **0.5**). Lower values make the agent more focused and predictable; higher values make it more creative and less predictable.

For support, appointments, and operational agents, keep temperature low. Save higher temperatures for creative or exploratory use cases.

### Call Direction

**Call Direction** decides how the agent participates in the calling flow:

- **Outbound** — the agent makes outgoing calls (campaigns, reminders, confirmations, follow-ups). This is the default for a new agent.
- **Inbound** — the agent answers incoming calls (support, reception, customer service).
- **Both** — the same agent works for incoming and outgoing calls.

<Warning>
  **Common mistake:** Leaving a new agent on **Outbound** when it should answer calls. An outbound agent doesn’t answer incoming calls, even with a number assigned. Set it to **Inbound** or **Both**.
</Warning>

## Section 2: Workflow

The **Workflow** tab is where you decide how the conversation moves. While the **Agent** tab defines who the assistant is, **Workflow** defines the path the call follows: branches based on what the caller wants, transfers, tool calls, and clean endings.

You build it on a visual canvas by placing nodes and connecting them. Use the buttons at the top left to zoom in, zoom out, and fit the whole flow on screen, and the buttons at the top right to go full screen or delete the workflow.

<Frame caption="Screenshot: a workflow with two routes from Start. The panel on the right shows what's still missing and the settings of the selected node.">
  <img
    src="/images/agentesia3.png"
    alt="The Workflow tab with a Start node connected to a Subagent and a phone number Transfer node, an End node, the Complete your workflow setup panel, and the Subagent settings on the right." />
</Frame>

### What you can build here

From a single starting point, you can:

- Create multiple routes inside the same conversation.
- Send the call to a **Subagent** for a specialized stage.
- Transfer the call to another agent.
- Transfer the call to a phone number.
- Run a tool that already exists.
- End the conversation with an **End** node.
- Control which route applies using **LLM conditions**.

### Adding nodes

To add a node, drag a connection from an existing node (for example, from the bottom of **Start**) and drop it on an empty part of the canvas. You can also click the **+** button below a selected node. A menu opens with the node types: **Subagent**, **Agent transfer**, **Phone number transfer**, **Tool**, and **End**.

The new node appears connected to the previous one, and its settings open in the panel on the right.

<Frame caption="Video: build a workflow with two routes from Start: a Subagent that books appointments and then ends the call, and a transfer to a phone number.">
  <video autoPlay muted loop playsInline src="/images/ai-agents-workflow.mp4" />
</Frame>

### Start

**Start** is the entry point. Every conversation begins here. The node doesn't speak or act — it just launches the route and sends the call to the next node.

The side panel for **Start** has two tabs:

- **Overview** — explains its role.
- **Edges** — lists every outgoing route from **Start**, with its label and destination.

### Transitions and LLM conditions

A transition is the line that connects one node to the next. Click its label (at first, *Configure condition*) to open its settings. The **Forward** tab defines the transition:

- **Transition type** — **LLM Condition** (the model decides when to take this route), **None**, or **Expression** (not yet editable in this panel).
- **Label** — the name shown on the canvas.
- **LLM condition** — a short text that tells the model when this route should apply.

Examples of conditions:

- "Use this route when the user needs technical help with the platform."
- "Use this route when the user asks to speak with a person."
- "Use this route when the issue is fully resolved and the conversation should end."

### Complete your workflow setup

The **Complete your workflow setup** panel lists exactly what's missing before the workflow can be saved. Each item shows the node or edge to fix and what it needs. Until the list is empty, **Save** stays disabled.

Common items it flags:

- An **Agent transfer** node without a target agent.
- A **Phone number transfer** node without a destination type.
- A **Tool** node without a tool selected.
- A transition with no LLM condition text.

### Subagent

A **Subagent** is a specialized stage inside the same workflow. Use it when one part of the conversation needs its own goal, voice, model, or behavior, different from the main agent. Give it a name at the top of the panel, then configure its tabs:

- **General** — **Conversation Goal** (instructions that extend the main prompt for this stage), **Override prompt** (to replace the main prompt instead of extending it), **Voice**, **LLM**, **Eagerness**, and **Spelling patience**.
- **Knowledge Base** — **Inherit knowledge base** is on by default. Turn it off to use a different one.
- **Tools** — **Inherit custom tools** is on by default. Use **Add Tool** to attach specific tools.
- **Edges** — the routes that leave this node.

Tools themselves are not created here. They're created in the global **Tools** section and only selected or inherited from inside the workflow.

### Tool node

The **Tool** node (shown as **Tool dispatch** in the side panel) sends the conversation to a stage where the agent uses an existing tool. Use **Add Tool** in its **Tools** tab to select at least one. This node doesn't create new tools — it just points the workflow to tools that already exist.

### Agent transfer

**Agent transfer** hands the conversation over to another agent in your account. Use it when the call should move to an assistant specialized in another task, language, or workflow.

You can configure:

- **Agent** — the target agent.
- **Delay (ms)** — how long to wait before the transfer, in milliseconds.
- **Transfer message** — an optional message the agent says during the transfer.
- **Enable first message** — whether the target agent plays its greeting after the transfer.

Examples of transfer messages:

- "I'm going to transfer you to the right agent for this request."
- "I'll connect you with the assistant specialized in this type of question."

### Phone number transfer

**Phone number transfer** sends the call out to a phone number. Use it when the conversation has to leave the agent and continue on a specific phone line.

The **Destination type** can be:

- **Phone number** — a fixed number, ideal for reception, a sales line, or a dedicated support line.
- **Phone (dynamic variable)** — the destination comes from a dynamic variable decided at runtime.

### End

The **End** node closes the conversation: the call ends when this node is reached. Use it when:

- The issue is fully resolved.
- A branch should close cleanly.
- A stage is complete and shouldn't continue.

### Duplicate and delete

When you select a node, two icons appear next to it to duplicate or delete it. **Duplicate** is handy for building similar branches faster. Before deleting, check the transitions connected to that node so you don't break a live route.

### Global settings

When no node is selected, the right panel shows **Global settings**. **Prevent infinite loops** stops the workflow from continuously transitioning in a loop when all conditions are true.

To disable a workflow without deleting it, disconnect the **Start** node. Without a connected start, the flow has no entry point and won't run.

<Warning>
  **Common mistake:** A workflow that looks complete on the canvas can still fail in practice. Test every route with a real conversation before publishing.
</Warning>

## Section 3: Knowledge Base

The **Knowledge Base** tab gives your agent extra background information to answer questions. It's best for large or detailed content — like manuals, FAQs, or policies — that doesn't fit in the main prompt.

<Frame caption="Screenshot: the Knowledge Base tab. Add the documents this agent can use to answer questions.">
  <img
    src="/images/agentesia4.png"
    alt="The Knowledge Base tab of an agent with the Add Document button and the list of documents the agent can use." />
</Frame>

### What this tab is for

This tab connects documents from your global Knowledge Base to a specific agent. It does **not** create content — that's done in the Knowledge Base manager.

Quick access:

- [Open the Knowledge Base manager](https://app.callin.io/knowledge-base)

A useful way to think about it:

- The **prompt** defines how the agent should behave.
- The **knowledge base** defines the reference content the agent can pull from when answering.

### When to use a knowledge base

Use it when the content is:

- Long (manuals, policies, internal documentation).
- Frequently updated.
- Already published as articles or pages.
- Better kept separate from the prompt for easier maintenance.

Typical sources: FAQs, product guides, support articles, policy documents, internal references.

### Supported content sources

The Knowledge Base manager organizes content in two types of sources:

- **Files** — uploaded documents.
- **Web** — published pages or online documentation.

### What you see when a base is linked

The tab lists every document the agent can use. If there are none yet, it shows *No documents added*.

### Main actions

Click **Add Document** to open the document picker:

- Use the search box to find a document from your Knowledge Base and select it to make it available to this agent.
- If the document doesn't exist yet, click **Add Document** at the bottom of the picker. It takes you to the Knowledge Base manager, where you can create it.

### Select Knowledge Base panel

In the Knowledge Base manager you can switch between **Files** and **Web**, search your content, and use **Create Knowledge base** to organize documents into groups. Once a document is there, it appears in the agent's document picker.

### Recommended flow

The cleanest way to work is to keep creation and selection separate:

<Steps>
  <Step>
    Create and organize your documents in the Knowledge Base manager.
  </Step>
  <Step>
    Come back to this tab and add the documents the agent needs.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  Keep behavior rules in the prompt and reference content in the Knowledge Base. That separation makes updates much easier later.
</Tip>

## Section 4: Conversation

The **Conversation** tab lists every call handled by this agent, incoming and outgoing. It's where you review what happened on each call. It has two sub-tabs: **Conversations** (phone and web calls) and **Google Meet Sessions**.

<Frame caption="Screenshot: the Conversation tab. Search transcripts and filter the agent's calls.">
  <img

### Search and filters

- **Search transcripts…** — find calls by what was said during the conversation.
- **Filters** — narrow the list by **Phone Number**, **Call ID**, **Squad**, **Status**, **Direction**, **Appointment**, **Call Transfer**, **Voicemail**, **Date Range**, **Call Duration**, **Origin**, **Campaign ID**, **Sentiment**, or **Ticket ID**.

Until the agent makes or receives its first call, the tab shows *No incoming or outgoing calls yet*.

### Google Meet Sessions

This sub-tab lists the Google Meet meetings the agent has joined (with the **Join Meet** button at the top of the editor). Search by the meeting URL or join code, and filter the list by **Status**. If the agent hasn't joined any meeting, you'll see *No Google Meet sessions found*.

<Tip>
  After changing the prompt or the workflow, place a few test calls and review them here. Reading the transcripts is the fastest way to spot where the agent goes off track.
</Tip>

## Section 6: Tools

The **Tools** tab decides what your agent can actually *do* during a call beyond having a conversation. While the prompt defines how it talks and the workflow defines how the call moves, tools let the agent take action at the right moment.

The tab has two sub-tabs: **Tools**, for your custom tools and the built-in **System Tools**, and **MCP**, for MCP servers.

<Frame caption="Screenshot: the Tools tab. Attach custom tools on the left and turn system tools on or off on the right.">
  <img
    src="/images/agentesia5.png"
    alt="The Tools tab with the Add Tool button on the left and the System Tools list on the right: Smart Hang-up, Language Switching, Intelligent Listening, Transfer to agent, Transfer to number, Voicemail Intelligence, and Access Property Data." />
</Frame>

Quick access:

- [Open the global Tools section](https://app.callin.io/tools)

### Custom tools

**Custom tools** are tools your team builds for specific use cases. They're not created inside the agent — first you build them in the global **Tools** section, and then you come back here to attach them.

Typical things a custom tool can do:

- Query an external system.
- Run an automation.
- Send or receive data through a **webhook** (a URL your tool calls when something happens).
- Trigger business-specific logic.
- Connect the agent to another service.

The **Add Tool** button opens a picker where you can search your tools and select one. If your account has no tools yet, the picker offers **Add Webhook Tool** to create one.

### MCP servers

The **MCP** sub-tab lets you connect MCP (Model Context Protocol) servers to give your agent additional tools and integrations without building them one by one.

<Frame caption="Screenshot: the MCP sub-tab. Connect MCP servers to the agent.">
  <img
    src="/images/ai-agents-mcp.png"
    alt="The MCP sub-tab of the Tools tab with the Add MCP button." />
</Frame>

Click **Add MCP** to search the MCP servers available in your account and connect one to the agent. If there are none yet, click **Manage MCPs** to set them up.

### System Tools

**System Tools** are built-in tools that handle common call situations without you having to build anything. Each one can be turned on or off. A new agent starts with three of them on — **Smart Hang-up**, **Language Switching**, and **Voicemail Intelligence** — and the counter at the top of the list shows how many are active.

Active tools with extra settings show a gear icon. Every tool's settings panel includes:

- **Description** *(optional)* — explains to the model how and when to use the tool. Leave it blank to use the default optimized prompt.
- **Disable interruption** — prevents the caller from interrupting while the tool is running.

#### Smart Hang-up

Lets the agent politely end the call once the conversation goal is met or the caller says goodbye. Its description already includes examples of explicit endings ("bye", "that's all", "no thanks") and implicit ones (minimal answers once the caller's needs are met, "I need to go"). This avoids keeping the line open longer than needed.

#### Language Switching

Lets the agent recognize and switch to the caller's preferred language during the call. It only works with the languages you added under **Language** in the **Agent** tab.

#### Intelligent Listening

Keeps the agent quiet when the caller says things like "give me a second" or "hold on", preventing awkward interruptions while they check something.

#### Transfer to agent

Hands the call off to a more specialized AI agent when the caller's request falls outside the current agent's expertise — for example, from a generic assistant to a sales agent.

When you turn it on, its panel includes **Transfer Rules**. Click **Add Rule** to define each one:

- **Agent** — the agent to transfer to.
- **Condition** — when this transfer should happen.
- **Delay before transfer (milliseconds)** — how long to wait before transferring.
- **Transfer message** — the exact phrase spoken before transferring.
- **Enable First Message** — whether the target agent plays its greeting after the transfer.

#### Transfer to number

Connects the caller with a live team member when a human touch is needed or the caller asks for it — reception, a switchboard, sales, or a specific office.

#### Human Transfer Rules

The **Transfer to number** panel includes **Human Transfer Rules**, where you define the conditions for transferring to human operators. Click **Add Rule** for each one:

- **Destination Type** — a fixed **Phone Number** or a number provided through a dynamic variable.
- **Phone Number** — the number to transfer to.
- **Condition** — when this rule should apply.

Use **Remove Rule** to delete one. Use a fixed number when the destination is always the same, and a dynamic variable when it depends on the conversation context.

#### Voicemail Intelligence

Recognizes when a call reaches an automated inbox and acts accordingly. This is critical for outbound agents, where calls often end up at voicemail.

In its settings, **Voicemail Message** *(optional)* decides what happens:

- Leave it blank to end the call immediately when voicemail is detected.
- Write a message (for example, "Hello, this is an automated call. Please call us back at your convenience. Thank you.") to play it before ending the call.

The gear icon only appears while **Voicemail Intelligence** is on. Click it to open **Edit Voicemail Detection Tool**, where you'll find **Voicemail Message**.

<Warning>
  **Common mistake:** Writing the voicemail message in the **Conversation Prompt** and leaving **Voicemail Message** empty. The agent hangs up as soon as it detects voicemail and doesn't say the message in your prompt. Write the message in **Voicemail Message**, or turn **Voicemail Intelligence** off if you want the agent to follow your prompt instead.
</Warning>

#### Access Property Data

Lets the agent use configured HTTPS XML feed URLs with property listings during the conversation. It's designed for real estate agents that need to answer questions about available properties. It's off by default.

#### Description and Disable interruption

As mentioned above, every system tool's panel includes **Description** and **Disable interruption**. Write a description only when you need the tool to behave differently from the default — for example, to tell **Smart Hang-up** not to end the call until a booking is confirmed.

### Choosing the right tools

Don't enable everything. Pick what fits your use case:

- An inbound support agent often uses **Smart Hang-up**, **Language Switching**, and **Intelligent Listening**.
- An agent that escalates to humans uses **Transfer to number** with clear transfer rules.
- An outbound agent often uses **Voicemail Intelligence**, **Smart Hang-up**, and **Language Switching**.

<Warning>
  **Common mistake:** Enabling too many tools at once. It makes behavior harder to predict, harder to test, and harder to maintain.
</Warning>

## Section 7: Call Action

The **Call Action** tab is where the agent stops being just a voice and starts producing useful outcomes: bookings, follow-up emails, tickets, structured data, and synced records — during and after the call.

<Frame caption="Screenshot: the Call Action tab. Turn on the actions your team will actually use.">
  <img
    src="/images/agentesia6.png"
    alt="The Call Action tab with toggles for Appointment Scheduling, Post-Call Summary Email, Smart Opt-Out, and Issue & Complaint Tracking, the Data Sync webhook field, and the Summary Language selector." />
</Frame>

Quick access:

- [Set up Google Calendar](https://app.callin.io/actions/google-calendar)
- [Set up Cal.com](https://app.callin.io/actions/cal)

### Appointment Scheduling

Lets the agent book meetings directly into your calendar during the call. Especially useful for sales, onboarding, and any flow where booking on the spot saves you from chasing the customer afterwards.

When you turn it on, the **Scheduling Configuration** panel asks for a **Scheduling Provider**:

- **Google Calendar** — best when you want to book directly into a connected Google calendar.
- **Cal.com** — best when your booking operation already runs on Cal.com.

Then select the calendar and the booking template the agent should use. You can create several booking templates per calendar and assign templates from different calendars to different agents.

<Warning>
  The agent's time zone and the calendar's time zone must match. If they don't, the agent will offer or confirm wrong times.
</Warning>

### Post-Call Summary Email

Automatically sends the call details to the email addresses you choose as soon as the conversation ends, so owners or teams stay in the loop without manual follow-up.

When you turn it on, the **Post Call Email Configuration** panel opens. Choose a template type:

- **Default template** — fill in **Recipients** (type an address and press Enter or click **Add**), **Subject**, and **Message**.
- **Custom template** — write the whole email in HTML in **Template body**. Use **Add Variables** to insert call data such as `userName`, `phoneNumber`, `direction`, `agent_name`, `duration`, `extractedDataHtml`, `keyMomentsHtml`, `transcriptHtml`, and `rawDataHtml`. **Format HTML** tidies the code and **Preview Email** shows the result.

You can also set:

- **Email Language** — the language of the email.
- **Include Call Data** — adds the call data to the email.
- **Include Extracted Fields** — adds the data captured with *Information Capture* (see below).

Once configured, a gear icon next to the toggle reopens the panel.

### Smart Opt-Out

Gives callers a polite way to decline future contact. Important for outbound flows: it keeps your lead lists clean and your outreach compliant with communication standards.

### Issue and Complaint Tracking

Detects issues or complaints during the call and automatically creates a ticket, so your team can review, follow up, or escalate it from the **Tickets** section. Turn it on when you want each conversation to be able to create a case worth handling afterwards.

### Data Sync (Post-Call Webhook)

Sends the call details and captured data to another system the moment the call ends. The right choice when you work with a CRM, an automation platform, or any internal tool that needs the data without manual copy-paste.

To set it up, enter the webhook URL that should receive the data, and confirm in advance which system will receive it and how it will process it.

### Summary Language

Sets the language of the conversation summaries. The default, **Auto (infer from conversation)**, writes each summary in the language the call was held in. Pick a fixed language from the searchable list if your team always needs summaries in the same one.

### Information Capture (Data Extraction)

Turns the conversation into structured data — names, dates, preferences, cities, products of interest, contact reasons, and so on.

Click **Add Entity** to define each piece of data to capture. The **Configure Data Extraction** panel asks for:

- **Data Type** — **String**, **Boolean**, **Number**, or **Integer**.
- **Identifier** — the internal name used to store it.
- **Description** — exactly what the system should extract. It's passed to the model, so describe in detail how to find it in the transcript.
- **Enum Values** *(optional)* — a closed list of values the model can choose from. If you don't add any, the model can use any value.

The counter above the button shows how many entities you've defined.

### Capture and Store Contact Data

When this is on, the extracted information gets saved into the contact's profile. Useful when you want to reuse this data on future calls or keep richer records of each contact.

### Call Retry

**Persistence & Connection (Call Retry)** sets up automatic retries when a call isn't answered or hits voicemail. Mostly used in outbound agents.

You can configure:

- **Retry Attempts (Retry Count)** — how many times the system should try again before stopping (default **0**, no retries).
- **Wait Time Between Tries (Retry Delay)** — how many minutes to wait between attempts (default **30**).

<Tip>
  Only enable the actions that your team will actually use. Each automation you turn on is a small commitment to maintain. Fewer, well-chosen actions beat a long list of half-used ones.
</Tip>

## Section 9: Advanced

The **Advanced** tab is where you fine-tune the agent: how it captures audio, how it handles silence and waiting times, how it manages call data, and how it performs in outbound telephony scenarios.

Most agents work fine without touching anything here. Open this tab when you want more precision, or when something specific isn't quite working.

<Frame caption="Screenshot: the Advanced tab. Most agents work well with the default values.">
  <img
    src="/images/agentesia8.png"
    alt="The Advanced tab showing the Memory block with Remember callers, the Voice & Understanding block with Speech Capture Quality and Priority Keywords, and the Conversation Flow block with Eagerness and Spelling patience." />
</Frame>

The tab is split into seven blocks: **Memory**, **Voice & Understanding**, **Conversation Flow**, **Background sound**, **Soft timeout**, **Privacy & Data**, and **Telephony Extras**.

### Memory

**Remember callers** saves useful details after each call and uses them the next time the agent recognizes the same person. By default, each person is recognized by the phone number they called from. It's off by default.

<Tip>
  Turn it on for agents that talk to the same customers often, such as support or appointment agents, so callers don't have to repeat themselves.
</Tip>

### Voice & Understanding

Adjusts how the agent captures audio and how it prioritizes specific terms.

#### Speech Capture Quality

Sets the audio format used to process speech. Options include **PCM 8000 Hz**, **PCM 16000 Hz** (*Recommended*), **PCM 22050 Hz**, **PCM 24000 Hz**, **PCM 44100 Hz**, **PCM 48000 Hz**, and **ulaw 8000 Hz**.

For most setups, leave it on **PCM 16000 Hz**. It's the best balance between audio quality and processing speed. **ulaw 8000 Hz** is specifically designed for telephony scenarios.

#### Priority Keywords

Lets you list, separated by commas, terms the agent should prioritize when recognizing speech. Useful for brand names, product names, technical terms, acronyms, codes, or unusual surnames the model might otherwise mishear — for example *SolarMax, AcmeCorp, 401k, deductible*.

### Conversation Flow

Controls the rhythm of the agent — how fast it joins in, how patient it is with slow speakers, and when it should hang up after silence.

#### Eagerness

How quickly the agent joins the conversation after the caller stops talking:

- **Patient** — waits longer before responding.
- **Normal** — balanced (default).
- **Eager** — responds quickly.

**Normal** works for most cases.

#### Spelling patience

Makes the agent more patient when the caller is slowly spelling something — names, emails, ID numbers — so it doesn't cut them off mid-letter. Options:

- **Auto** — the default, recommended when the agent often collects spelled-out information.
- **Off** — keeps standard timing.

#### Take turn after silence

How many seconds the agent waits after the caller stops speaking before taking its turn (default **7**). Lower values feel snappier; higher values give the caller more room to think.

#### End conversation after silence

How many seconds of silence before the call ends automatically when the caller stops responding. It helps manage costs and keeps your call logs clean. The default, **-1**, means the call never ends because of silence.

#### Max conversation duration

The maximum length of a single call, in minutes (default **10**). A safety net to keep calls concise and prevent them from running indefinitely.

<Tip>
  If you're not sure what to set in the silence and duration fields, leave them at their default and only adjust them after testing real calls.
</Tip>

### Background sound

Plays a continuous background sound during the conversation, mixed with the agent's voice. Turn on **Enable background sound** and set:

- **Sound** — **Office (Quiet)** (default), **Office (Busy)**, **Restaurant**, **City**, **Typing**, and several **Elevator** options. Use the play button to preview it.
- **Volume** — the volume of the sound relative to the agent's voice (default **60%**).
- **Crossfade loop** — smooths the point where the sound loops, to avoid audible pops.

### Soft timeout

Gives the caller immediate audio feedback when the model takes longer than expected to generate a response, so the line never feels dead. Turn on **Enable soft timeout** and set:

- **Timeout duration** — how many seconds to wait for the model's response before playing a message (default **3**).
- **Use LLM Generated Message** — generates a contextual filler phrase with the model.
- **Message** — the fallback phrase used if no filler is generated in time (default *"Hhmmmm...yeah."*).
- **Additional filler messages** — use **Add message** to add phrases for the 2nd, 3rd, and later soft timeouts in the same response, so the agent doesn't repeat itself.

### Privacy & Data

#### Record Calls

On by default. Securely stores the transcripts and audio of every call, so you can review the agent's performance, stay compliant, and keep a history of your customer interactions. Turn it off only if it's not compatible with your internal privacy, storage, and compliance policies.

### Telephony Extras

Telephony-specific settings, mostly aimed at improving outbound call performance.

#### Number Rotation

Automatically cycles through your available phone numbers. Helps prevent a single number from being flagged as spam, distributes call volume, and improves deliverability. It requires phone numbers in your account: if there are none, a warning asks you to add them first.

#### Select Phone Numbers and Calls Per Number

When **Number Rotation** is on and your account has numbers, you choose:

- **Select Phone Numbers** — which numbers will rotate. At least one is required.
- **Calls Per Number** — how many calls to make from one number before switching to the next.

#### State-Based Restriction

Increases answer rates by making the agent dial each contact with a phone number from their own state. Currently it supports US local numbers only — confirm your account has compatible local numbering before enabling it.

<Warning>
  Only change Advanced settings when you have a clear reason and can test the result with real calls. Random tweaks here usually create new problems instead of solving the old one.
</Warning>
