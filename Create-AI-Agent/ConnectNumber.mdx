## Connect your existing number to an agent

If your business already has a phone number, you don’t need to rebuild your phone setup for an AI agent to answer it. In Callin.io there are three ways to do it:

- **Call forwarding**: keep your current line and forward calls to the agent’s number.
- **Porting**: move your existing number to Callin.io so the agent answers it directly.
- **BYOC (SIP Trunk)**: connect your own carrier, SIP trunk, or PBX.

The best option depends on whether you want speed, to keep your number, or an advanced technical setup.

## Where each option is managed

- **Numbers** ([open it here](https://app.callin.io/numbers)): buy numbers, start a porting request, and see all your numbers.
- **BYOC** ([open it here](https://app.callin.io/byoc)): connect Telnyx or another SIP provider.
- **AI Agents**: assign a number to each agent.

All three are in the left menu, under **Build** (AI Agents) and **Telephony** (BYOC and Numbers).

## Option 1: Keep your current number and use call forwarding

This is the fastest and simplest way to get started.

Your main number stays with your current phone provider. You get a number in Callin.io, assign it to your agent, and forward your business number to it. When someone calls your main line, the agent answers.

### How it works

<Steps>
  <Step>
    In **Numbers**, buy a number or add one you already have (see [Numbers And BYOC](/Create-AI-Agent/Configure-Numbers)).
  </Step>
  <Step>
    Assign that number to your agent (see [Assign the number to the agent](#assign-the-number-to-the-agent)).
  </Step>
  <Step>
    With your phone carrier, forward your business number to the agent’s number.
  </Step>
</Steps>

From then on, your customers keep calling the number they already know, and the agent handles the call.

<Warning>
  Call forwarding is set up with your phone carrier, your phone, or your PBX, not inside Callin.io. Some carriers let you do it from their customer portal; others need a request, and forwarded calls may have a cost depending on your plan.
</Warning>

### When this option makes sense

- You want to start quickly.
- You want to keep your public number without changes.
- You don’t want to migrate the number yet.

### Practical example

A clinic has used the same landline for years. It appears on its website and on Google. The clinic buys a number in **Numbers**, assigns it to its agent, and asks its carrier to forward the main line to it. Patients keep calling the same number, and the agent answers.

## Option 2: Port your number

Porting moves your current number to Callin.io (“Keep your existing number”). Your customers keep calling the same number, without depending on forwarding.

  <video autoPlay muted loop playsInline src="/images/connect-number-porting.mp4" />
</Frame>

### Start a porting request

<Steps>
  <Step>
    Go to [Numbers](https://app.callin.io/numbers) and click **\+ Add Number** → **Purchase Number**.
  </Step>
  <Step>
    In **Country**, choose the country of your number.
  </Step>
  <Step>
    In **Request Type**, select **Porting**.
  </Step>
  <Step>
    Enter your number in **Porting Number** in international format (example: `+34910000000`) and click **Check Portability**.
  </Step>
  <Step>
    If the number can be ported, click **Start request** and complete the **Document Requirements** for that country.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  **Porting** only appears for countries that use requests (outside the US and Canada). For the US and Canada, the window shows the instant purchase instead.
</Note>

<Frame caption="Screenshot: the portability check. When the number can be ported, you see its carrier, number, and type, and you can click Start request.">
  <img src="/images/porting1.png" alt="Purchase a New Number window for Spain with Porting selected, an example number in Porting Number, the message This number is eligible for porting with the Carrier Name, Phone Number and Type, and the Start request button" />
</Frame>

### What the check tells you

- **This number is eligible for porting**: the request can continue. You see the **Carrier Name**, the **Phone Number**, and its **Type** (for example, `local`).
- If there is no coverage for that number, the window tells you, and the request can’t continue. That doesn’t mean the number is invalid, only that it can’t be ported through this flow.

After you click **Start request**, the process is the same as for a new number: documents, fees, and review. You can follow it in the **Number Requests** tab. See [Numbers And BYOC](/Create-AI-Agent/Configure-Numbers) for details.

<Tip>
  Porting takes longer than buying a number, because your current provider must release it. Don’t cancel your current line until the number appears in your **Numbers** list. If your number can’t be ported, call forwarding is still the fastest way to start.
</Tip>

### Practical example

A law firm has used the same number for years and its clients know it. With porting, the firm keeps it as its main contact number and the agent answers it directly, without forwarding.

## Option 3: Connect your infrastructure with BYOC (SIP Trunk)

BYOC means **Bring Your Own Carrier**. You connect your own telephony provider to Callin.io, so the agent works on top of the infrastructure you already have.

It is the most advanced option, for businesses that already use VoIP, a PBX, or their own SIP provider.

<Frame caption="Video: BYOC → Telnyx tab → Other SIP Provider tab with its Basic, Inbound, and Outbound tabs.">
  <video autoPlay muted loop playsInline src="/images/connect-number-byoc.mp4" />
</Frame>

### BYOC with Telnyx

If you already use Telnyx:

<Steps>
  <Step>
    Go to [BYOC](https://app.callin.io/byoc) and open the **Telnyx** tab.
  </Step>
  <Step>
    Paste your **Telnyx API Key** and click **Start**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Callin.io saves the key, fetches your active Telnyx numbers, stores them in your account, provisions the SIP trunk, and creates the phone numbers for inbound and outbound calls. Your Telnyx numbers then appear in **Numbers**.

### BYOC with another SIP provider

If your provider is not Telnyx, open the **Other SIP Provider** tab (**Generic SIP Trunk / PBX Extension (Any Provider)**). It works with a trunk number (DID) and with a PBX extension.

- **Basic**: a **Label**, the **Phone Number / Extension**, and whether it **Supports inbound** and **Supports outbound**.
- **Inbound**: where calls reach your system (**Inbound SBC / FQDN**, **Transport**, and **Media encryption**).
- **Outbound**: how calls leave Callin.io (**Outbound SBC / FQDN**, **Transport**, **Media encryption**, **Username**, **Password**, and **Allowed countries (outbound)**).

Click **Save & Import** when you finish. Callin.io then checks that the number can be used for calling.

<Warning>
  BYOC is recommended for businesses that already have a telephony team, a SIP provider, or a PBX. For every field, see [Numbers And BYOC](/Create-AI-Agent/Configure-Numbers#byoc-sip-trunk).
</Warning>

### Practical example

A company already uses Telnyx for its business lines. Instead of buying new numbers, it connects Telnyx in **BYOC**, imports its numbers, and assigns them to its agents.

## Assign the number to the agent

Having the number in your account is not the last step. The agent only uses a number that is assigned to it. In **Numbers**, an unassigned number shows **This number is not assigned to any agent**, and in **AI Agents** an agent without a number shows **No phone number assigned**.

<Steps>
  <Step>
    Go to **AI Agents**.
  </Step>
  <Step>
    Click the **⋮** menu of the agent and select **Assign Number**.
  </Step>
  <Step>
    In the **Assign Number to** window, find the number (you can search by number, label, or carrier) and click **Assign**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Frame caption="Screenshot: the Assign Number window. The Status column shows whether each number is free or already assigned to another agent.">
  <img src="/images/connect-number-assign.png" alt="Assign Number window for an agent, with the search box, the Filters and Refresh buttons, and the list of numbers with their status, carrier and Assign button" />
</Frame>

<Warning>
  **Common mistake:** Assigning a number that another agent is using. As the window warns, **assigning it here will detach it from the other agent**. If you have one agent for support and another for sales, check that each line is assigned to the right one.
</Warning>

## Which option should you choose

- **To start quickly without changing your main number**, use call forwarding.
- **To keep your number and have the agent answer it directly**, use porting when it’s available.
- **If you already have your own telephony infrastructure**, use BYOC.

The best option is the one that fits your current setup, your provider, and the level of control you want.
